2. Background 72
The use of voice-activated technology and similar devices for assisting elderly 73
individuals and those with type 2 diabetes has garnered widespread international interest. 74
While substantial efforts have been made to develop these technologies for healthcare 75
enhancement, their adoption remains limited, highlighting the need for more comprehensive 76
initiatives to fully utilize their potential. 77
Despite many possible applications, evidence on the efficacy of these technologies in 78
the elderly population is limited. Therefore, we developed a model of behavioral intervention 79
delivered through the Smart Speaker Echo Dot 3rd Gen (Amazon Alexa) to promote 80
knowledge and skills necessary for mental health promotion and diabetes self - management in 81
this population. 82
83
3. Research question 84
For elderly individuals with type 2 diabetes, what is the effect of an interactive virtual 85
assistance system activated by voice delivered through Smart Speaker EchoDot 3rd Gen 86
(Amazon Echo®) in: 87
1. Mental health; 88
2. Quality of life; 89
3. Diabetes self-care behavior; 90
4. Glycemic profile; 91
5. Blood pressure; and 92
6. Lipid profile. 93

11. Sample size 359
Sample size calculations were performed using the Statistics and Sample Size Pro 360
software (6 ). With an alpha level of 0.05, a power of 90%, and an effect size of 0.68 (Cohen's 361
d), it was estimated that 94 subjects (47 per group) would be required to assess mean 362
differences in the prima ry outcome. Assuming a 20% dropout rate, we plan to include 112 363
subjects (56 per group) in the study. The effect size of 0.68 was based on the randomized 364
controlled trial published by Fulmer R et al. (7 ), which evaluated the efficacy of using 365
psychological artificial intelligence software to reduce self - identified symptoms of depression 366
and anxiety. 367

12.2. Primary and secondary outcome analysis 387
All analysis will be performed after the end of the trial and no interim analysis will be 388
performed. The analysis of all study outcomes will be carried out using analysis of 389
covariance (ANCOVA), as all the outcomes are quantitative. This approach will allow a 390
comparison of means between groups, while also considering potential variations in 391
covariates. As we will compare the means at the end of the study, rather than deltas 392

18
(differences from baseline), the model will include baseline data of the outcome variable as a 393
covariate. This adjustment allows us to account for the initial variation in scores among the 394
groups before assessing any po tential intervention effects. 395
All analysis will be performed using the intention-to-treat approach, including all 396
subjects who were randomized. Per protocol analysis will only be performed as a sensitivity 397
analysis, as described in the section 12.3. Normality tests for outcome data will not be 398
performed. Instead, we will conduct visual histogram and QQ plot analyses. If distributional 399
assumptions are in doubt, we will perform a logarithmic transformation of the data before 400
analysis. 401

12.3. Missing data 403
The imputation of missing data will be handled following the European Medicines 404
Agency Guideline on Missing Data in Confirmatory Clinical Trials ( 10 ). The overall rate of 405
missing data at week 12 is expected to be no more than 20% and the frequency and reasons 406
for missing data are expected to be missing at random (MAR). However, the possibility of 407
missing not at random (MNAR) cannot be excluded, as we believe that three factors may 408
influence the missing data: 409
1. Participants less familiar with technology may have a higher tendency to dropout 410
and might experience reduced benefits due to a higher likelihood of non - adherence to the 411
intervention. 412
2. Participants from the usual care group may have a higher tendency to discontinue 413
participation due to not rece iving the device as they expected. 414
3. Older participants, who are less likely to experience intervention-related benefits, 415
may be more likely to miss the final evaluation due to potential mobility challenges and a 416
greater reliance on caregiver assistance t o attend the consultation. 417
Therefore, to explore the potential impact of missing data in different scenarios, 418
promoting a better understanding of how different methods of handling missing data impact 419
our results considering the challenge of handling missin g data within the possibility of 420
MNAR assumption, three distinct imputation strategies will be employed to assess the impact 421
of missing data on our results: 422
1 . Multiple imputation with baseline data correction: we will perform multiple 423
imputation of missing data correcting for the baseline data. This correction aims to mitigate 424
potential bias that could be introduced by missing data imputation using multiple imputation 425
under a possible MNAR assumption. 426

19
2. Baseline Observation Carried Forward (BOCF): given our anticipation and belief 427
that both control and intervention subjects are likely to exhibit improvement regarding the 428
baseline parameters, we will adopt the B OCF technique as a sensitivity analysis. By carryin g 429
forward the baseline observation, our analysis will be conducted within a more conservative 430
scenario that tends toward the null hypothesis, reducing the probability of type 1 error related 431
to missing data imputation. 432
3. Exclusion of participants with missing data: furthermore, we will conduct a 433
sensitivity analysis considering only the trial completers. As this analysis has the potential to 434
introduce selection bias, the results will be treated as hypothesis generation as subjects with 435
missing data may dif fer in certain aspects from those without missing data. 436
437
12.4. Adjustment for covariates 438
For adjustment, age, sex, mini-mental state examination score, education, income, and 439
baseline data of the outcome variable will be included in the ANCOVA model as covariates. 440
Additionally, other variables that were found to be imbalanced between groups at baseline 441
will also be included into the analysis. 442
443
12.5. Sensitivity and subgroup analysis 444
Sensitivity analysis will be conducted to assess the impact of missing data as 445
described above. A lso, pre - specified subgroup analysis will be performed: age (< 80 vs. ≥ 80 446
years old), education level (graduate vs. undergraduate), sex (male vs. female), insulin usage 447
(yes vs. no), history of depression (yes vs. no) and cognitive impairment (yes vs. no, 448
accordingly to MMSE score adjusted for education level). Due t o the increased risk of type I 449
and type II error and reduced power, only subgroup analysis for primary outcome will be 450
performed. 451
452
12.6. Adjustment for multiple testing 453
No adjustment for multiple testing will be performed. Therefore, all outcomes rather 454
than primary outcome will be considered hypothesis generation and p - value will not be 455
reported. 456
